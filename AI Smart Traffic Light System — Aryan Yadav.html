A fixed timer can’t see the road.

A fixed-duration signal gives every road the same green time, whether it has a long queue or no cars. Here the demo runs in fixed-timer mode: every 7 seconds the signal swaps, regardless of demand.

One intersection. Four approaches.

North, South, East and West share one signal. The two opposing pairs, North–South and East–West, take turns. A yellow and an all-red interval separate each switch, so conflicting roads are never green together.

Teaching a camera to count what matters.

A Raspberry Pi camera on a corner pole could view all four approaches. The boxes in the scene are an illustrative overlay, not live detections from a real camera.

The emergency override.

An ambulance approaches from the East. Follow the proposed decision sequence as it unfolds live:

  1. Ambulance detected
  2. Emergency override active
  3. Conflicting traffic stopped
  4. Yellow and all-red interval
  5. Green for the ambulance road
